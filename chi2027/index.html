Whose

A eureka is the moment something new becomes clear. When people and AI make discoveries together, we ask whose moment it is: new to whom, whose understanding changed, and who steered the inquiry and gets the credit.

Human-AI Co-Discovery of Knowledge

The workshop will be open to CHI 2027 attendees, with priority for authors of accepted contributions; participants without papers are welcome in all discussions, and no prior reading is required.

Contact: zj2445@cumc.columbia.edu

About

As AI systems take on more active roles in knowledge work, they create new opportunities to formulate questions, uncover unexpected connections, and develop explanations. Yet a plausible or useful finding does not necessarily deepen people's understanding of its evidence or implications.

This workshop explores human-AI co-discovery of knowledge: how people and AI shape inquiry, develop and challenge ideas, and make sense of emerging findings across scientific research and other forms of knowledge work, including design and learning. Our title question, Whose Eureka?, asks what is new and for whom, whose understanding changes, and how inquiry is directed and contributions are recognized.

We bring together researchers and practitioners in human-AI interaction, visual analytics, AI for science, design, and learning to identify shared challenges, ethical considerations, and research priorities for designing and evaluating AI-supported discovery.

We hope to invite research, thoughts, or provocations on questions such as:

  • How do people and AI develop questions, connections, and explanations together? How interaction helps people recognize the significance of an unexpected connection and decide whether to pursue it, rather than simply receive a suggestion, and how explanations remain open to challenge as new evidence is considered.
  • How do people guide co-discovery, and how are contributions recognized? When to intervene, change direction, or decline assistance; how records of key choices and evidence help people question the path to a finding; and how credit, intellectual ownership, and responsibility are negotiated.
  • What do people come to understand through co-discovery? How inquiry with AI changes how people frame a problem, what they notice, and what they ask next, even when an investigation produces no finding, and how such changes in understanding can be recognized.
  • What counts as a discovery, for whom, and on what evidence? How criteria for novelty, evidence, and value differ across research, design, learning, and everyday knowledge work, and whether AI-supported inquiry broadens or narrows the questions a community pursues.

Call for Participation

Topics

We plan to invite empirical studies, systems, design concepts, critical perspectives, and work in progress, especially concrete examples of discovery with AI, including unsuccessful ones. Topics include:

  • Interpreting data and existing knowledge together
  • Serendipity and unexpected connections
  • Constructing and challenging explanations
  • Human agency in directing inquiry and delegating to AI
  • Human understanding and learning
  • Evidence, uncertainty, and unsuccessful investigations
  • Contributions, credit, and responsibility
  • Novelty and value across contexts

Speakers and provocations

If the workshop is accepted, we will invite a keynote and two invited speakers whose work spans areas such as AI for science, learning, visual analytics, and design; they will also join the roundtables as discussants. Facilitators will open each roundtable with a provocation for participants to agree or disagree with, for example:

  • Serendipity cannot survive an AI optimized for relevance.
  • If an AI proposed the key idea, the human “eureka” is mostly recognition.
  • A discovery that no one on the team can explain is not yet a discovery.
  • Co-discovery tools should sometimes make inquiry slower.
  • AI-assisted discovery will make research communities converge on the same questions.
