Whose A eureka moment is when something suddenly becomes clear. When people and AI make a discovery together, we ask: Is it new to a person, a team, or a field? Whose understanding changes? Who guides the inquiry, and who gets the credit?

Human-AI Co-Discovery of Knowledge

An illustrative human-AI co-discovery process

AI systems increasingly help formulate ideas, explore data, and develop explanations. Yet a plausible or useful finding does not necessarily expand or deepen human knowledge. Discovery and understanding can reinforce one another: an unexpected connection may change how people frame a problem and what they ask next.

This workshop explores human-AI co-discovery of knowledge: how people and AI develop new questions, connections, and explanations together. We invite researchers and practitioners to discuss agency, evidence, learning, and how contributions are recognized, and to help shape a shared research agenda for discovery with AI.

  • Q1Co-discovery process How do people and AI develop questions, connections, and explanations together?

    People and AI can build on, challenge, or redirect one another's suggestions as they explore evidence and develop provisional conclusions. How can human-AI interaction help people recognize when an unexpected connection is worth pursuing, rather than simply accepting an AI suggestion?

  • Q2Agency & credit How do people guide co-discovery, and how are contributions recognized?

    As people delegate work to AI, they decide when to intervene, revise a plan, or decline assistance. Records of decisions and revisions can make human and AI contributions visible. How should people negotiate credit, intellectual ownership, and responsibility for discoveries that emerge from human-AI collaboration?

    Related reading

    • Principles of Mixed-Initiative User Interfaces Horvitz · CHI 1999
    • Cocoa: Co-Planning and Co-Execution with AI Agents Feng et al. · CHI 2026

  • Q3Human understanding What do people come to understand through co-discovery?

    Human-AI co-discovery may change how people frame a problem, what they notice, and what they ask next, even when an investigation remains inconclusive. How can we recognize reflection, learning, and changes in understanding alongside the findings that people and AI produce?

    Related reading

    • Do People Engage Cognitively with AI? Impact of AI Assistance on Incidental Learning Gajos and Mamykina · IUI 2022

  • Q4What counts as discovery What counts as a discovery, for whom, and on what evidence?

    Is an insight new to an individual, a team, or a field? Do its claims hold up against counter-evidence or alternative explanations? Concerns about scientific monocultures, where research converges on similar ideas and approaches, also raise a question: can co-discovery broaden the questions a community pursues?

Program

The tentative program spans two 90-minute sessions, with a keynote, lightning talks, a human-AI panel, and interactive roundtables. Poster exchange will take place during the conference coffee break.

  1. Welcome 5 min

  3. Lightning talks: first block 25 min
  4. Human-AI panel discussion 30 min

Conference coffee break and poster exchange

Session 2: Co-Discovery 90 min

  1. Lightning talks: second block 20 min
  2. Interactive roundtable activity 50 min
  3. Group reports and collective discussion 15 min
  4. Closing and next steps 5 min

Organizers

Explore the team’s research and its connections to the workshop questions.

  • Zhihan Jiang

    Columbia University

    • Human-AI co-sensemaking
    • Visual analytics

    Her research focuses on human-AI co-sensemaking, examining how people interpret complex data and develop their own understanding through interaction with AI. She has built visual analytics tools for exploring children's health through multimodal data and a knowledge-empowered AI assistant for dietary monitoring. She has also proposed student-AI collaborative inquiry for learning.

  • Yifang Wang

    Florida State University

    • Visual analytics
    • AI for science

    Her research connects data visualization, human-AI collaboration, and AI agents for scientific discovery and decision-making. She develops systems that help researchers express intent, explore complex information, and guide evolving analyses. Her visual analytics platforms also help analyze the science ecosystem and inform science policy.

  • Ruishi Zou

    Harvard University

    • Visual sensemaking
    • Knowledge communication

    His research explores how intelligent, interactive interfaces can help people make sense of complex texts and data. He is also interested in how graphics can help people communicate knowledge.

  • Shiyu Xu

    University of Michigan

    • Evidence interpretation
    • Collaborative learning

    She studies human-centered intelligent systems for sensemaking in everyday and clinical settings. Her interests include how narratives and visual representations help people interpret evidence, and how collaborative learning supports reflection and understanding.

  • Rui Sheng

    Northeastern University

    • Visual analytics
    • AI-assisted analysis

    Rui's research combines human-AI collaboration and visual analytics to support scientific inquiry and reasoning with complex information. His work examines how interactive representations help people inspect computational processes, assess evidence, and diagnose failures in AI-assisted analysis.

  • Samuel Schmidgall

    Google DeepMind

    • Research agents
    • Scientific evaluation

    His research focuses on AI for scientific discovery, including agent-based research workflows and the evaluation of AI judgments about scientific ideas. He studies how human guidance can shape automated research workflows and improve the quality of their outcomes.

  • Chenhao Tan

    University of Chicago

    • Hypothesis generation
    • Research evaluation

    His recent work focuses on AI for scientific discovery. His work includes hypothesis generation, AI scientists, and methods for evaluating and verifying scientific research outputs.

  • Xuhai “Orson” Xu

    Columbia University

    • Human-AI interaction
    • Explainable AI

    His research spans human-AI interaction, ubiquitous computing, and health informatics. He models behavior with everyday sensor data and designs AI agents and explanations to support decisions by individuals and experts. He examines how intelligent interfaces shape people's understanding, reliance, and decision-making, including how these effects vary with expertise.
