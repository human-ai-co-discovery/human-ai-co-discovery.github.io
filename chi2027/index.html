Human-AI Co-Discovery of Knowledge

Call for Participation View Program

An illustrative human-AI co-discovery process

AI systems increasingly help formulate ideas, explore data, and develop explanations. Yet a plausible or useful finding does not necessarily expand or deepen human knowledge. Discovery and understanding can reinforce one another: an unexpected connection may change how people frame a problem and what they ask next.

This workshop explores human-AI co-discovery of knowledge: how people and AI develop new questions, connections, and explanations together. We invite researchers and practitioners to discuss agency, evidence, learning, and how contributions are recognized, and to help shape a shared research agenda for discovery with AI.

An illustrative scenario · Step 1 of 3

From an experience to a question

The researcher brings an account of notification overload. The AI asks whether notifications help people finish faster.

What changes: An experience becomes a question to investigate together.

Still open: The interview excerpt alone cannot answer that question.

An illustrative scenario · Step 2 of 3

A closer look changes the story

The researcher notices that experienced users seem to receive more notifications. The AI points out that, within each experience group, more notifications are linked to slower work.

What changes: Looking within groups changes the interpretation of the overall trend.

Still open: This association does not establish that notifications cause slower work.

An illustrative scenario · Step 3 of 3

An explanation opens the next question

The researcher suggests that experience may mask interruption costs and asks whether the pattern holds across task types. The AI proposes a controlled study.

What changes: The next question considers how task and context shape notification experiences.

Still open: The proposed explanation needs testing.

Find your way into the questions

Open a question for more detail and related reading.

  • Q1Co-discovery process How do people and AI develop questions, connections, and explanations together?

    People and AI can build on, challenge, or redirect one another's suggestions as they explore evidence and develop provisional conclusions. How can human-AI interaction help people recognize when an unexpected connection is worth pursuing, rather than simply accepting an AI suggestion?

    Related reading

    • How AI Processing Delays Foster Creativity: Exploring Research Question Co-Creation with an LLM-based Agent Liu et al. · CHI 2024
    • CollabCoder: A Lower-barrier, Rigorous Workflow for Inductive Collaborative Qualitative Analysis with Large Language Models Gao et al. · CHI 2024
    More readings for Q1 →
  • Q2Agency & credit How do people guide co-discovery, and how are contributions recognized?

    As people delegate work to AI, they decide when to intervene, revise a plan, or decline assistance. Records of decisions and revisions can make human and AI contributions visible. How should people negotiate credit, intellectual ownership, and responsibility for discoveries that emerge from human-AI collaboration?

    Related reading

    • Principles of Mixed-Initiative User Interfaces Horvitz · CHI 1999
    • Cocoa: Co-Planning and Co-Execution with AI Agents Feng et al. · CHI 2026
    More readings for Q2 →
  • Q3Human understanding What do people come to understand through co-discovery?

    Human-AI co-discovery may change how people frame a problem, what they notice, and what they ask next, even when an investigation remains inconclusive. How can we recognize reflection, learning, and changes in understanding alongside the findings that people and AI produce?

    Related reading

    • Do People Engage Cognitively with AI? Impact of AI Assistance on Incidental Learning Gajos and Mamykina · IUI 2022
    • Artificial Intelligence and Illusions of Understanding in Scientific Research Messeri and Crockett · Nature 2024
    More readings for Q3 →
  • Q4What counts as discovery What counts as a discovery, for whom, and on what evidence?

    Is an insight new to an individual, a team, or a field? Do its claims hold up against counter-evidence or alternative explanations? Concerns about scientific monocultures, where research converges on similar ideas and approaches, also raise a question: can co-discovery broaden the questions a community pursues?

    Related reading

    • How Do Agents Fail on AutoResearch: End-to-End Diagnostic Evaluation on 100 Real-World Frontier Research Tasks Fei et al. · arXiv 2026
    • Artificial intelligence tools expand scientists’ impact but contract science’s focus Hao, Xu, Li, and Evans · Nature 2026
    More readings for Q4 →

We welcome position papers, empirical studies, system descriptions, design concepts, critical perspectives, and work in progress on human-AI co-discovery.
