Whose Eureka?

Human-AI Co-Discovery of Knowledge

StatusThis workshop is proposed for CHI 2027 and is under review; decisions are expected in November 2026. If it is accepted, the call for participation, important dates, and submission instructions will be posted on this page.

01About

Finding something new is not the same as understanding it

As AI systems take on more active roles in knowledge work, they create new opportunities to formulate questions, uncover unexpected connections, and develop explanations. Yet a plausible or useful finding does not necessarily deepen people's understanding of its evidence or implications.

This workshop explores human-AI co-discovery of knowledge: how people and AI shape inquiry, develop and challenge ideas, and make sense of emerging findings across scientific research and other forms of knowledge work, including design and learning. Our title question, Whose Eureka?, asks what is new and for whom, whose understanding changes, and how inquiry is directed and contributions are recognized.

We bring together researchers and practitioners in human-AI interaction, visual analytics, AI for science, design, and learning to identify shared challenges, ethical considerations, and research priorities for designing and evaluating AI-supported discovery.

02Open questions

Four connected questions for discussion

  1. How do people and AI develop questions, connections, and explanations together?

    How interaction helps people recognize the significance of an unexpected connection and decide whether to pursue it, rather than simply receive a suggestion, and how explanations remain open to challenge as new evidence is considered.

  2. How do people guide co-discovery, and how are contributions recognized?

    When to intervene, change direction, or decline assistance; how records of key choices and evidence help people question the path to a finding; and how credit, intellectual ownership, and responsibility are negotiated.

  3. What do people come to understand through co-discovery?

    How inquiry with AI changes how people frame a problem, what they notice, and what they ask next, even when an investigation produces no finding, and how such changes in understanding can be recognized.

  4. What counts as a discovery, for whom, and on what evidence?

    How criteria for novelty, evidence, and value differ across research, design, learning, and everyday knowledge work, and whether AI-supported inquiry broadens or narrows the questions a community pursues.

03Participate

Planned call for participation

We plan to invite empirical studies, systems, design concepts, critical perspectives, and work in progress on topics including:

  • Interpreting data and existing knowledge together
  • Serendipity and unexpected connections
  • Constructing and challenging explanations
  • Human agency in directing inquiry and delegating to AI
  • Human understanding and learning
  • Evidence, uncertainty, and unsuccessful investigations
  • Contributions, credit, and responsibility
  • Novelty and value across contexts
Submissions
Papers of 2–4 pages in ACM double-column format, excluding references.
Review
A program committee will review contributions for relevance, quality, and discussion potential.
Sharing
With permission, accepted papers and presentation materials will be shared on this website and, if authors choose, indexed on arXiv. There are no separate archival proceedings.
Dates & submission
To be announced here if the workshop is accepted.
04Program

Two in-person sessions

We expect about 35 participants, including organizers. The workshop will be open to CHI 2027 attendees, with priority for authors of accepted contributions; participants without papers are welcome in all discussions, and no prior reading is required.
