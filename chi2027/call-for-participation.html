About the Workshop

This workshop explores human-AI co-discovery: how people and AI develop new questions, connections, and explanations together. A plausible or useful finding does not necessarily deepen people's understanding. An insight may also be new to a person or team without being new to a field.

We bring together researchers and practitioners from HCI, AI, and related fields to compare experiences of discovery with AI, including unsuccessful investigations. Through the four questions below, we will identify shared challenges, ethical considerations, and research priorities for supporting both new findings and human understanding. Our aim is to build a community and a shared research agenda for human-AI co-discovery.

Contributions

What to contribute

We welcome contributions that offer a clear perspective, concrete experience, or open question for discussion:

  • Position papers and critical perspectives on unresolved questions, tensions, or alternative visions for human-AI co-discovery.
  • Empirical studies and accounts of practice examining how interaction shapes questions, interpretations, findings, or human understanding.
  • System descriptions and design concepts that support exploration, explanation building, evidence assessment, reflection, or human agency.
  • Work in progress and case descriptions, including unsuccessful investigations that reveal limitations, unexpected behavior, or lessons for future inquiry.

Concrete examples of discovery with AI are especially welcome. A contribution might trace how an initial idea changed, what led people to challenge an interpretation, or what they learned even when the investigation remained inconclusive.

Topics and questions

Contributions may address one or more of the four workshop questions and the directions under each:

How do people and AI develop questions, connections, and explanations together?
  • Interpreting together. How people and AI make sense of data and existing knowledge, formulate questions, and revise interpretations as evidence develops.
  • Serendipity and unexpected connections. How interaction with AI shapes the recognition of unexpected patterns, relationships, and possibilities, and how people decide which connections are worth pursuing.
  • Developing and testing explanations. How people and AI construct, compare, challenge, and revise explanations, models, and representations, including the assumptions that underpin them.

How do people guide co-discovery, and how are contributions recognized?
  • Human agency in co-discovery. How people direct inquiry, work with proactive AI agents, delegate tasks, and decide when to intervene, redirect an exchange, or pursue another direction.
  • Whose contribution, whose discovery? How human and AI contributions become visible, and how credit, intellectual ownership, and responsibility are understood and negotiated.

What do people come to understand through co-discovery?
  • Human understanding and learning. What people come to understand through co-discovery, how that understanding changes over time, and how reflection and further questioning develop even when an investigation remains inconclusive.

What counts as a discovery, for whom, and on what evidence?
  • Evidence, uncertainty, and failure. How people and AI seek counter-evidence, recognize misleading patterns, work with uncertainty, and learn from unsuccessful inquiry.
  • What counts as a discovery? What makes a connection or finding new and worthwhile for an individual, a team, or a field, and how novelty and value vary across research and practice.

At the Workshop

The program and invited speakers are tentative, subject to workshop acceptance, speaker availability, and the conference schedule.

  • Keynote and human-AI panel. We plan a keynote with Q&A by Daniel McDuff (Google Research) and a 30-minute panel with Daniel McDuff, Sherry Tongshuang Wu, Toby Jia-Jun Li, and an AI agent. The agent will respond to the same questions as the human panelists and to attendees' follow-up questions. Participants can challenge its assumptions, examine the evidence behind its claims, and compare its reasoning with the human panelists' perspectives.
  • Lightning talks and posters. Talks by accepted contributors will be split into two blocks, with about three minutes per talk and timings adjusted to the final program. During the conference coffee break, authors are encouraged to display posters on their own devices.
  • Interactive roundtable activity. Seven groups with participants from different disciplines will explore a question with AI, using public or participant-approved materials. Organizers will facilitate. Each group will identify a turning point or unresolved difficulty and reflect on it through the four workshop questions. Participants will choose and approve any excerpts shared beyond their group.
  • Group reports and collective discussion. Each group will give a report of up to two minutes, sharing an exchange, insight, or unresolved difficulty and an open question. A brief discussion will compare recurring themes and differences across the groups.

After the Workshop

With authors' permission, accepted papers and workshop materials will remain available on this website, including an index of papers that authors choose to post on arXiv. We do not plan separate archival proceedings.

Interested participants may also contribute to a follow-up perspective or research-agenda paper for an HCI venue, with authorship reflecting substantive contributions to synthesis and writing.

We will create an opt-in Discord community for discussion, resource sharing, and connections before, during, and after the workshop. It will be open to interested researchers and practitioners, whether or not they attend.

Accessibility

You can contribute through speech or writing, and facilitators will include both in the discussion. Instructions and prompts will be available in accessible electronic formats and in print. Presenters will use readable text and provide written and spoken descriptions of visuals.

For access requests, contact zj2445@cumc.columbia.edu. We will coordinate with participants and the CHI Accessibility Chairs.
