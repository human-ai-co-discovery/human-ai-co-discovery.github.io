    A roundtable provocation

    What do you think?

    “A discovery that no one on the team can explain is not yet a discovery.”

    Choose a position to explore an argument and a question it raises. Your choices are not saved or submitted.

    Understanding is part of discovery

    From this perspective, discovery involves human understanding alongside novelty and evidence.

    Then ask: What must someone be able to explain—the finding, its evidence, or the underlying mechanism?

    A finding can come before an explanation

    A finding may count as a discovery if it is new and well supported, even when an explanation is still missing.

    Then ask: What evidence would be enough to count it as a discovery?

    The kind of claim matters

    Your answer may depend on the domain, how the finding will be used, and what “explain” means.

    Then ask: Would a new pattern, a causal claim, and a design insight need the same kind of explanation?

    Try another position to explore a different perspective, or bring a follow-up question to the community.
