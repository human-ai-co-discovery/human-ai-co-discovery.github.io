Program

Schedule

We plan two 90-minute in-person sessions. The program is tentative and subject to workshop acceptance, speaker availability, and the conference schedule.

Session 1: Perspectives 90 min

  1. Welcome 5 min
  2. Keynote with Q&A 30 min
  3. Lightning talks: first block 25 min
  4. Human-AI panel discussion 30 min

Conference coffee break and poster exchange

Session 2: Co-Discovery 90 min

  1. Lightning talks: second block 20 min
  2. Interactive roundtable activity 50 min
  3. Group reports and collective discussion 15 min
  4. Closing and next steps 5 min

Session 1: Perspectives

  1. 5 min

    Welcome

    An overview of the workshop's goals and activities.

  2. 30 min

    Keynote with Q&A

    A keynote by Daniel McDuff (Google Research), followed by audience questions.

  3. 25 min

    Lightning talks: first block

    Authors of accepted contributions give brief talks of around three minutes each.

  4. 30 min

    Human-AI panel discussion

    Daniel McDuff joins Sherry Tongshuang Wu, Toby Jia-Jun Li, and a planned AI panelist for a moderated discussion with audience questions.

Conference coffee break and poster exchange

We encourage authors to display posters on their own devices during the conference coffee break.

Session 2: Co-Discovery

  1. 20 min

    Lightning talks: second block

    The second block continues the talks by accepted contributors, with around three minutes per talk.

  2. 50 min

    Interactive roundtable activity

    Participants form seven cross-disciplinary groups to explore a question with AI. Each group identifies a turning point or unresolved difficulty and reflects on how its questions, explanations, or understanding developed, guided by the four workshop questions.

  3. 15 min

    Group reports and collective discussion

    Each group gives a report of up to two minutes, followed by a collective discussion of recurring themes and differences.

  4. 5 min

    Closing and next steps

    Organizers summarize the discussion and outline opportunities for continued exchange and collaboration after the workshop.

Invited Guests (tentative)

Keynote speaker and panelist

  • Daniel McDuff

    Google Research

    Daniel builds machine learning systems to sense, model, and synthesize human behavior, with a focus on health, physiology, and affective computing. His research spans camera-based physiological measurement, wearable foundation models, and health agents that draw personal and clinical insights from longitudinal sensor data.

Panelists

  • Sherry Tongshuang Wu

    Carnegie Mellon University

    Sherry is an Assistant Professor in the Human-Computer Interaction Institute at Carnegie Mellon University. Her research connects HCI and natural language processing to help people work with imperfect AI through interactive debugging and correction. Her work has been published at ACL and CHI, and in TOCHI and TVCG.

  • Toby Jia-Jun Li

    University of Notre Dame

    Toby is an Assistant Professor of Computer Science and Engineering at the University of Notre Dame. He leads the SaNDwich Lab and directs the Human-Centered Responsible AI Lab. He designs and studies interactive systems that help people create, configure, and extend AI-powered systems, including human-AI collaboration for the future of work.

  • An AI agent

    An AI agent is planned as the fourth panelist. It will respond to the same questions as the human speakers and to follow-up questions from attendees. Participants can challenge its assumptions, examine the evidence behind its claims, and compare its reasoning with the human panelists' perspectives.
