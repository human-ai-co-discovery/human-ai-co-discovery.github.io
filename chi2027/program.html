Proposed CHI 2027 workshop · Pittsburgh, PA, USA

Program

Whose Eureka? Human-AI Co-Discovery of Knowledge

We plan two consecutive 90-minute in-person sessions. The schedule and speaker arrangements are tentative, subject to workshop acceptance and the conference schedule.

Session 1: Perspectives

  1. 5 min

    Welcome

    Workshop goals and overview of activities.

  2. 30 min

    Keynote with Q&A

    Daniel McDuff, Google Research (tentative).

  3. 25 min

    Lightning talks: first block

    Brief talks by authors of accepted contributions.

  4. 30 min

    Human-AI panel discussion

    Tentative panelists: Daniel McDuff, Sherry Tongshuang Wu, Toby Jia-Jun Li, and an AI agent.

Conference coffee break and poster exchange

Authors are encouraged to share posters on their own devices during the conference coffee break to reduce printing.

Session 2: Co-Discovery

  1. 20 min

    Lightning talks: second block

    Brief talks by authors of accepted contributions.

  2. 50 min

    Interactive roundtable activity

    Cross-domain group inquiry with AI, including reflection and discussion.

  3. 15 min

    Group reports and collective discussion

    Short group reports followed by comparison of recurring themes and differences.

  4. 5 min

    Closing and next steps

    Summary and opportunities for continued exchange after the workshop.

Keynote and panel

Daniel McDuff, Senior Staff Research Scientist at Google Research, is supportive of joining as keynote speaker, subject to workshop acceptance and his availability. Sherry Tongshuang Wu and Toby Jia-Jun Li are supportive of joining him on the panel. These arrangements remain tentative.

  • Daniel McDuff · Google Research · Keynote and panel
  • Sherry Tongshuang Wu · Carnegie Mellon University · Panel
  • Toby Jia-Jun Li · University of Notre Dame · Panel
  • An AI agent · Planned fourth panelist

The AI agent will respond to the same questions as the human speakers and engage with attendee follow-ups. Participants can challenge its assumptions, examine its evidence, and compare its reasoning with the human panelists' perspectives. A moderator will guide the exchanges and invite audience participation.

How participants will take part

Lightning talks

Authors of accepted contributions will give brief talks in two blocks, targeting three minutes each, with the duration adjusted to the final program.

Interactive roundtable activity

Participants from different disciplines will form seven discussion groups. Organizers will facilitate, with invited speakers and other organizers joining as discussants. Using public or participant-approved materials, groups will explore a question with AI.

Each group will identify a turning point or unresolved difficulty, reflect on how its questions, explanations, or understanding developed, and consider what further evidence would be needed. Participants will choose and approve any excerpts shared beyond their group.

Reflection and discussion are included in the 50-minute activity, guided by the four workshop questions. Participants may also reflect on their contributions as explorers, challengers, connectors, or verifiers. Prompts include:

  • If an AI proposed the key idea, the human eureka is mostly recognition.
  • Co-discovery tools should sometimes make inquiry slower.

Group reports and collective discussion

Each group will give a report of up to two minutes, highlighting an exchange, insight, or unresolved difficulty, together with an open question. A brief collective discussion will compare recurring themes and differences across groups.

After the workshop

With contributors' permission, accepted papers and workshop materials will remain available on the website, including an index of papers that authors choose to post on arXiv. Organizers will invite interested participants to develop a perspective or research-agenda paper for an HCI venue, with authorship based on contributions to the synthesis and manuscript.

The opt-in Discord community will support continued discussion, resource sharing, research updates, and future connections, including researchers and practitioners beyond the workshop.
